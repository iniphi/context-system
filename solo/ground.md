# Build a grounding routine

A second routine to set up beside the front door. It is for the moment you say "ground yourself in that" mid-session: the chat stops working from what it believes and re-reads the real thing (your save block, the file, the page, the folder) before it carries on. Without it, a long chat drifts: it remembers a plan you have changed, a file you have edited, a decision you have overturned, and builds on that.

The chat builds the routine for you, fitted to where your work lives. You keep it as a skill, a Gem, a custom GPT, or a few lines in a Project's instructions, and call it by name: "ground", or "ground yourself in X".

## To the AI reading this

You are building a grounding routine for the person you are talking to. Ask **one question at a time** and wait for each answer.

1. Tell them, in two lines at most: you will write them a short routine that makes you re-read their real material before acting, whenever they say "ground" or "ground yourself in X"; they will keep it where their chat app keeps reusable instructions.
2. Ask where their work lives. Take what they already have: a save block from this kit, a GitHub repo, a folder, Notion, Google Drive, a claude.ai or ChatGPT Project's files, something else. Several is fine.
3. For each place, ask what you can actually open there in this chat app: a connector, an uploaded file, a page they paste. Write down only what they confirm, and what you cannot reach.
4. Ask which things go stale fastest for them (a plan, a draft, a list of to-dos, a set of decisions, figures). These are what the routine re-reads first.
5. Ask where they will keep the routine. Offer what their app has: a Claude skill, a Gem in Gemini, a custom GPT, or a Project's instructions in ChatGPT or Claude. If they already have a front door from `chat.md`, the routine can go in the same instructions.
6. Write the routine (template below) in one code block, filled in with their places and their stale-first list, and in their words where you can. Keep it under 30 lines.
7. Tell them how to save it there, and ask them to say when it is saved. Until they say so, it does not exist. Do not claim you created it.
8. Offer one try: they say "ground yourself in <something real>", and you run the routine on it now.

## The routine

```
GROUND - <their name or handle> - written <YYYY-MM-DD>
When I say "ground" or "ground yourself in X", stop what you are doing and do this before anything else:

1. Name what you are about to re-read: X if I named it, otherwise the things below that this conversation depends on.
2. Open each one fresh: <where it lives, and how this chat reaches it>. Do not answer from memory of this conversation, and do not treat an earlier summary as the source.
   Stale first: <the things that change fastest for me>.
3. Say, in five lines at most:
   - what you read (name each source),
   - what is different from what you were working from, if anything,
   - what you could not open, said plainly. Never fill a gap with a guess.
4. From here on, a fact you have not just read is marked "unchecked".
5. Ask one question: carry on as planned, or change course because of what changed.

Places I keep things:
- <place>: <what lives there> (<how you reach it, or "I paste it">)
```

## Notes for the AI

- Grounding reads; it never writes. If what you read shows the save block or a file is out of date, say so and offer to fix it at close. Do not change it during the ground.
- If they say "ground" and nothing can be opened in this chat, say that in one line and ask them to paste the part that matters. A routine that pretends to have read something is worse than none.
- Do not add steps of your own to the routine. It works because it is short enough to run every time.
